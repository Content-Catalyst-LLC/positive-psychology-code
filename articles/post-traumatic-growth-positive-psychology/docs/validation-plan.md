# Validation Plan

This plan is intended for professional research use with real, ethically collected trauma-adjacent data.

## Data validation

- Confirm data provenance and collection context.
- Confirm trauma context, event timing, chronicity, ongoing exposure, safety status, and measurement timing.
- Confirm scale direction and units.
- Check missingness by context, wave, participant, subgroup, item, and construct.
- Evaluate whether missingness reflects distress, mistrust, burden, dissociation, safety concerns, low access, language barriers, disability barriers, institutional vulnerability, or coercion.
- Inspect outliers and impossible values.
- Document inclusion, exclusion, transformation, and de-identification rules.

## Indicator validation

- Estimate reliability for PTG appreciation, relationships, personal strength, new possibilities, existential change, meaning-making, support, agency, distress, and well-being separately.
- Inspect correlations among disruption, rumination, meaning-making, support, agency, narrative integration, PTG domains, distress, and well-being.
- Test dimensional structure using PCA, factor analysis, or confirmatory modeling where appropriate.
- Avoid collapsing PTG domains before checking whether the five-domain structure matters.
- Report sensitivity to weighting and direction-correction choices.

## Model validation

- Test whether meaning-making, deliberate rumination, support, agency, and narrative integration predict PTG over time.
- Model distress separately from PTG.
- Test whether ongoing stress and intrusive rumination predict distress.
- Test whether social support buffers ongoing stress.
- Test whether perceived growth and corroborated growth align over time.
- Compare context-level patterns across illness, bereavement, violence, disaster, accident, and chronic stress contexts.

## Ethical validation

- Avoid interpreting lack of reported growth as personal failure.
- Avoid romanticizing trauma or implying suffering is necessary for development.
- Avoid forcing meaning-making before safety and stabilization.
- Avoid using PTG measures for high-stakes individual screening, ranking, discipline, hiring, benefits, legal, insurance, or clinical decisions.
- Treat trauma, recovery, distress, and growth data as highly sensitive.
- Document limits clearly in outputs and article-adjacent interpretation.
