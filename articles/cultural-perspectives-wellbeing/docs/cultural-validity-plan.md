# Cultural Validity Plan

A cross-cultural well-being study should document more than statistical reliability. It should document whether the construct, items, translations, and interpretation remain meaningful across cultural settings.

## Conceptual validity

- Define the well-being construct before measurement.
- Identify whether the construct is individual, relational, institutional, spiritual, ecological, or mixed.
- Document local terms and concepts related to happiness, dignity, harmony, duty, meaning, and flourishing.
- Include local researchers or community advisors when possible.

## Translation and adaptation

- Use forward and backward translation where applicable.
- Document culturally adapted language.
- Pilot items for clarity and local resonance.
- Check whether response scales make sense in context.
- Evaluate whether examples, metaphors, or affect terms carry unintended meanings.

## Measurement equivalence

- Test configural invariance when comparing factor structure.
- Test metric invariance before comparing relationships between variables.
- Test scalar invariance before comparing latent means.
- Use partial invariance or alignment approaches where full invariance is unrealistic.
- Report limitations clearly.

## Interpretation

- Avoid deficit framing of groups with lower scores.
- Avoid romantic framing of groups with high relational or cultural continuity scores.
- Interpret results with institutional, historical, ecological, and political context.
- Treat cultural values as dynamic and internally diverse, not static group essences.
