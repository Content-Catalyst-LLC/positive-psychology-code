# Method Notes

This companion scaffold treats explanatory style as a multidimensional attributional pattern shaped by events, cognition, agency, support, and context.

A working explanatory-burden model for bad events is:

```text
explanatory_burden =
  negative-event stability
+ negative-event globality
+ negative-event personalization
```

A working positive-event integration model is:

```text
positive_event_integration =
  positive-event stability
+ positive-event globality
+ positive-event internal/effort attribution
```

A working context-adjusted agency model is:

```text
context_adjusted_agency =
  agency
+ support
+ controllability
- setback_intensity
- explanatory_burden
```

A working resilience/persistence model is:

```text
persistence =
  agency
+ support
+ controllability
+ positive_event_integration
- explanatory_burden
- setback_intensity
```

## Recommended research workflow

1. Inspect missingness, distributions, and scale direction.
2. Direction-correct explanatory burden and distress where needed.
3. Evaluate reliability separately for negative-event attributions, positive-event attributions, agency/support, and outcomes.
4. Preserve stability, globality, and personalization before reducing to an explanatory-burden index.
5. Preserve bad-event and good-event attributions separately.
6. Measure setback intensity, controllability, support, institutional context, and actual opportunity.
7. Model persistence, hope, wellbeing, and distress separately where possible.
8. Use network models as exploratory attribution-system maps, not causal proof.
9. Interpret results in relation to family, school, workplace, health, culture, structural conditions, and institutional fairness.
10. Avoid treating explanatory-style scores as clinical measures, employment tools, school-ranking tools, disciplinary tools, benefits tools, or individual assessments.

## Scope and use

These examples use synthetic data for research workflow demonstration, methods development, documentation, and reproducible analysis. They are not designed for clinical diagnosis, treatment planning, therapeutic intervention, workplace screening, employment selection, student ranking, employee evaluation, benefits eligibility decisions, or individual psychological assessment.
