# Validation Plan

This plan is intended for professional research use with real, ethically collected explanatory-style data.

## Data validation

- Confirm data provenance and collection context.
- Confirm whether data are self-report, interview-based, diary-based, experience-sampled, behaviorally coded, or language-coded.
- Confirm domain, role, power relationship, event severity, measurement timing, and setting.
- Confirm scale direction and units.
- Check missingness by domain, wave, participant, subgroup, item, and construct.
- Evaluate whether missingness reflects burden, stigma, distrust, low access, language barriers, disability barriers, institutional vulnerability, trauma, or privacy concerns.
- Inspect outliers and impossible values.
- Document inclusion, exclusion, transformation, and de-identification rules.

## Indicator validation

- Estimate reliability for negative-event stability, globality, personalization, positive-event integration, agency, support, persistence, hope, wellbeing, and distress separately.
- Inspect correlations among attributions, setback intensity, controllability, agency, support, persistence, hope, wellbeing, and distress.
- Test dimensional structure using PCA, factor analysis, or confirmatory modeling where appropriate.
- Avoid collapsing bad-event and good-event explanations into a single optimism score too early.
- Report sensitivity to weighting and direction-correction choices.

## Model validation

- Test whether explanatory burden predicts lower persistence after setbacks.
- Test whether support buffers the association between explanatory burden and persistence.
- Test whether positive-event integration predicts hope and agency.
- Test whether controllability changes the interpretation of negative attributions.
- Test whether setback intensity predicts distress independently of attributional style.
- Compare domain-level patterns across education, work, health, community, transition, and sport contexts.

## Ethical validation

- Avoid interpreting pessimistic attributions as personal failure without evaluating actual constraints, repeated harm, discrimination, uncontrollability, threat, and institutional betrayal.
- Avoid using explanatory-style measures for high-stakes individual screening, ranking, discipline, hiring, benefits, or evaluation.
- Treat setback narratives, attribution patterns, distress, hope, and resilience data as sensitive.
- Document limits clearly in outputs and article-adjacent interpretation.
