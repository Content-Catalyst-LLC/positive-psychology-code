# Validation Plan

This plan is intended for professional research use with real, ethically collected character-strengths data.

## Data validation

- Confirm data provenance and collection context.
- Confirm whether data are self-report, peer-report, observed, interview-based, behaviorally coded, or derived from institutional systems.
- Confirm domain, role, power relationship, measurement timing, and setting.
- Confirm scale direction and units.
- Check missingness by context, wave, participant, subgroup, item, and construct.
- Evaluate whether missingness reflects role vulnerability, fear of evaluation, low trust, language barriers, disability barriers, institutional vulnerability, religious/cultural sensitivity, or privacy concerns.
- Inspect outliers and impossible values.
- Document inclusion, exclusion, transformation, and de-identification rules.

## Indicator validation

- Estimate reliability for the six virtue clusters and related constructs: signature-strength use, authenticity, contextual support, institutional suppression, overuse risk, flourishing, meaning, relationship quality, and engagement.
- Inspect correlations among 24 strengths, virtue clusters, signature-strength use, authenticity, support, suppression, overuse, and outcomes.
- Test dimensional structure using PCA, factor analysis, or confirmatory modeling where appropriate.
- Avoid collapsing the full strength profile into a single character score.
- Report sensitivity to weighting and direction-correction choices.

## Model validation

- Test whether signature-strength use predicts flourishing over time.
- Test whether contextual support moderates signature-strength effects.
- Test whether institutional suppression reduces authenticity or strength expression.
- Test whether strength overuse risk weakens the association between strengths and flourishing.
- Test whether virtue clusters relate differently to meaning, relationships, engagement, and wellbeing.
- Compare context-level patterns across education, work, community, transition, and leadership contexts.

## Ethical validation

- Avoid interpreting low strength scores as moral failure without evaluating context, cultural meaning, role constraints, safety, institutional suppression, or measurement fit.
- Avoid using strengths data for high-stakes individual screening, ranking, discipline, hiring, benefits, employee evaluation, or student evaluation.
- Treat character, spirituality, honesty, humility, leadership, moral identity, and strengths-profile data as sensitive.
- Document limits clearly in outputs and article-adjacent interpretation.
