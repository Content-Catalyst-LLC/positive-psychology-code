# Validation Plan

This plan is intended for professional research use with real, ethically collected learned-helplessness or perceived-control data.

## Data validation

- Confirm data provenance and collection context.
- Confirm whether data are self-report, behavioral, interview-based, diary-based, experience-sampled, observed, institutionally recorded, or experimentally derived.
- Confirm domain, role, power relationship, event severity, measurement timing, and setting.
- Confirm scale direction and units.
- Check missingness by domain, wave, participant, subgroup, item, and construct.
- Evaluate whether missingness reflects burden, distress, stigma, low trust, low access, language barriers, disability barriers, institutional vulnerability, trauma, or privacy concerns.
- Inspect outliers and impossible values.
- Document inclusion, exclusion, transformation, and de-identification rules.

## Indicator validation

- Estimate reliability for perceived control, uncontrollability, attributional stability, globality, internality, motivation, depressive symptoms, agency, support, mastery, recovery, feedback, and institutional fairness separately.
- Inspect correlations among control, uncontrollability, attribution, motivation, depressive symptoms, support, mastery, recovery, and institutional fairness.
- Test dimensional structure using PCA, factor analysis, or confirmatory modeling where appropriate.
- Avoid collapsing actual uncontrollability and perceived control into one score.
- Report sensitivity to weighting and direction-correction choices.

## Model validation

- Test whether perceived control predicts motivation and agency recovery.
- Test whether uncontrollable events predict helplessness and depressive-symptom burden.
- Test whether attributional stability, globality, and internality predict generalization.
- Test whether support buffers the association between helplessness and depressive symptoms.
- Test whether mastery experience and recovery opportunity predict restored agency.
- Compare domain-level patterns across education, work, health, community, transition, and public-system contexts.

## Ethical validation

- Avoid interpreting low agency as personal failure without evaluating actual constraints, repeated harm, discrimination, uncontrollability, threat, institutional betrayal, or lack of safe recovery pathways.
- Avoid using learned-helplessness measures for high-stakes individual screening, ranking, discipline, hiring, benefits, or evaluation.
- Treat adversity, perceived control, motivation, depressive symptoms, hopelessness, and recovery data as sensitive.
- Include appropriate clinical escalation protocols where severe hopelessness, depressive symptoms, or self-harm risk could appear.
- Document limits clearly in outputs and article-adjacent interpretation.
