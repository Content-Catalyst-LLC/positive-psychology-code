# Validation Plan

This plan is intended for professional research use with real, ethically collected flow and engagement data.

## Data validation

- Confirm data provenance and collection context.
- Confirm domain, session timing, task structure, and measurement timing.
- Confirm whether flow data were self-reported, experience-sampled, observed, inferred from behavior, or derived from task systems.
- Confirm scale direction and units.
- Check missingness by domain, session, participant, subgroup, item, and construct.
- Evaluate whether missingness reflects burden, disengagement, fatigue, mistrust, language barriers, disability barriers, institutional vulnerability, or surveillance concerns.
- Inspect outliers and impossible values.
- Document inclusion, exclusion, and transformation rules.

## Indicator validation

- Estimate reliability for challenge, skill, attention, feedback, clarity, meaning, autonomy, distraction, flow, performance, fatigue, recovery, and well-being separately.
- Inspect correlations among challenge, skill, balance, attention, feedback, goal clarity, meaning, autonomy, distraction, interruptions, flow, performance, learning, fatigue, recovery, and well-being.
- Test dimensional structure using PCA, factor analysis, or confirmatory modeling where appropriate.
- Avoid collapsing conditions, experience, and outcomes into a single engagement score.
- Report sensitivity to weighting and direction-correction choices.

## Model validation

- Test whether challenge-skill balance, attention, feedback, clarity, meaning, and autonomy predict flow.
- Test whether distraction, interruptions, and fatigue reduce flow.
- Test whether flow predicts performance, learning, and well-being.
- Test whether recovery moderates the flow-wellbeing relationship.
- Test whether attention protection and autonomy are stronger predictors in digital or technical-work contexts.
- Compare domain-level patterns across education, creative work, sport, technical work, health training, and digital work.

## Ethical validation

- Avoid interpreting low flow as personal failure without evaluating task design, skill support, interruption burden, fatigue, disability access, surveillance pressure, and institutional constraints.
- Avoid using flow measures for high-stakes individual screening, ranking, discipline, hiring, benefits, or evaluation.
- Treat attention, engagement, performance, productivity, and activity data as sensitive.
- Document limits clearly in outputs and article-adjacent interpretation.
